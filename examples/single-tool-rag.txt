Most travelers from the U.S., Canada, the U.K., the EU, Australia and many other countries can visit Japan without a visa for short‑term tourism—usually up to 90 days. Just bring a passport that’s valid for the duration of your stay (and often a bit beyond) and be prepared to show a return or onward ticket. Check the official Japanese consular site for the exact rules that apply to your passport.

When you’re in Tokyo (and the rest of Japan), a few etiquette basics will help you blend in smoothly:

* **Bowing** is the standard greeting and sign of respect; a slight bow is enough for casual encounters.  
* **Tipping** isn’t customary and can even be confusing, so you can leave a tip only if you’re at a very high‑end international hotel that explicitly accepts it.  
* **Quiet on public transport**—keep phone conversations low‑key and set phones to silent.  
* **Shoes off** when entering homes, traditional inns (ryokan), temples, and some restaurants; you’ll often be offered slippers.  
* **Queue orderly** at stations, elevators, and ticket counters; Japanese people value orderly lines.  

Following these simple customs will make your visit smoother and show respect for local culture. Safe travels!
