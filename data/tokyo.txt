DESTINATION GUIDE: TOKYO, JAPAN

Note: This is a simplified reference document created for a technical assessment exercise. It reflects general, commonly-known travel patterns and is not authoritative, up-to-date travel or visa advice.

VISA & ENTRY
Many nationalities — including most EU countries, the UK, the US, Canada, and Australia — can enter Japan visa-free for short tourist stays (typically up to 90 days), though exact allowances vary by passport. Travelers should have a valid passport with sufficient remaining validity and often a return/onward ticket.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
Spring (March–May) is popular for cherry blossoms, with mild temperatures. Autumn (September–November) offers similarly mild weather and autumn foliage. Summer (June–August) is hot and humid with a rainy season in June. Winter (December–February) is cool and dry, generally the least crowded season.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Bowing is a common greeting. Tipping is not customary and can even cause confusion. It's considered polite to be quiet on trains and to remove shoes when entering homes and certain traditional accommodations. Queuing in an orderly fashion is expected everywhere.

PACKING TIPS
Layered clothing works well given seasonal variation. Comfortable walking shoes are essential given how much travel is done on foot and via train stations. In summer, light breathable fabrics and a portable fan or cooling towel help with humidity; in winter, warm layers are needed as many older buildings are not centrally heated.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Japan is generally considered very safe with low crime rates. The main natural hazards are occasional earthquakes and, during typhoon season (roughly August–October), heavy rain and strong winds. Tap water is safe to drink in most areas.
