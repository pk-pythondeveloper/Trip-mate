DESTINATION GUIDE: BANGKOK, THAILAND

Note: This is a simplified reference document created for a technical assessment exercise. It reflects general, commonly-known travel patterns and is not authoritative, up-to-date travel or visa advice.

VISA & ENTRY
Many nationalities, including most EU countries, the UK, the US, Canada, and Australia, can enter Thailand visa-free or via visa-on-arrival for stays typically up to 30 days, though this varies by nationality and is subject to change. Travelers should check current requirements for their specific passport.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
The cool season (November–February) is generally considered the most comfortable, with lower humidity. The hot season (March–May) brings high temperatures. The rainy season (June–October) brings monsoon rains, often in short, heavy bursts rather than all-day rain.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Thailand places strong cultural importance on respect for the monarchy and Buddhist traditions; visitors should dress modestly when visiting temples (shoulders and knees covered) and remove shoes before entering. Public displays of anger are frowned upon, and the head is considered a sacred part of the body (avoid touching others' heads), while feet are considered the lowest/least respectful (avoid pointing feet at people or religious images).

PACKING TIPS
Lightweight, breathable clothing is recommended given the heat and humidity. A modest cover-up layer (long trousers/skirt, a shoulder-covering top) is useful for temple visits. A compact umbrella or rain jacket is handy during the rainy season.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Bangkok is generally safe for tourists, though petty theft and common tourist scams occur in busy areas. Tap water is generally not recommended for drinking; bottled or filtered water is preferred. Traffic can be heavy and pedestrian crossings should be navigated carefully.
