DESTINATION GUIDE: BARCELONA, SPAIN

Note: This is a simplified reference document created for a technical assessment exercise. It reflects general, commonly-known travel patterns and is not authoritative, up-to-date travel or visa advice.

VISA & ENTRY
Spain is part of the Schengen Area. Visitors from the EU, UK, US, Canada, and Australia typically do not need a visa for short tourist stays (usually up to 90 days within a 180-day period); requirements for other nationalities vary.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
Late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September–October) offer warm, pleasant weather without the peak summer crowds and heat. Summer (July–August) is hot and the most crowded/tourist-heavy season. Winter is mild compared to much of Europe but can be cool and occasionally rainy.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Meal times run later than in many countries — lunch is often early-to-mid afternoon and dinner frequently after 9 pm. A short rest period ("siesta") in the early afternoon is a cultural norm, though less universally observed in tourist areas. A modest, included service charge is common, though small additional tips for good service are appreciated but not obligatory.

PACKING TIPS
Comfortable walking shoes are recommended given how walkable the city is. Light layers work well for most of the year; a light jacket is useful for cooler evenings even in summer. Modest attire (shoulders covered) is expected when visiting churches such as the Sagrada Família.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Barcelona is generally safe but has a notable rate of pickpocketing and bag-snatching in tourist-heavy areas (public transport, Las Ramblas, beaches). Keeping valuables secure and being alert in crowds is commonly advised. Tap water is safe to drink.
