DESTINATION GUIDE: REYKJAVIK, ICELAND

Note: This is a simplified reference document created for a technical assessment exercise. It reflects general, commonly-known travel patterns and is not authoritative, up-to-date travel or visa advice.

VISA & ENTRY
Iceland is part of the Schengen Area. Visitors from the EU, UK, US, Canada, and Australia typically do not need a visa for short stays (usually up to 90 days within a 180-day period), though rules for non-Schengen visitors vary and should be checked based on nationality.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
Summer (June–August) offers mild temperatures and near-midnight sun, ideal for outdoor activities. Winter (December–February) is cold with very short daylight hours but is the best season for viewing the Northern Lights. Shoulder seasons (spring/autumn) offer fewer crowds but more unpredictable weather.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Icelanders are generally informal; first names are used often, even in professional settings. Tipping is not expected, as service charges are typically included. Punctuality is valued. Outdoor hot tub/pool etiquette requires showering (without swimwear) before entering.

PACKING TIPS
Weather can change rapidly regardless of season, so waterproof and windproof outer layers are strongly recommended year-round. Warm layers, a hat, and gloves are essential in winter. Sturdy waterproof footwear is recommended for exploring natural sites such as waterfalls and glaciers.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Iceland is considered very safe with very low crime rates. The main risks are weather and terrain related — sudden storms, icy roads in winter, and unpredictable conditions near cliffs, glaciers, and hot springs. It's advisable to check road and weather conditions before day trips outside Reykjavik.
